the plot of a feature film into a single
22minute episode? How could it feel so
chaotic and random and yet so
emotionally complete? The answer is a
surprisingly simple formula. It's an
eight-step blueprint that the show's
creator, Dan Harmon, uses for literally
every single episode. Hi guys, my name

blueprint is called the story circle and

by step. Once you see it, you'll start

structures. So, I'd love to hear in the

want me to cover. For now, let's get
into the framework that powers some of
the most inventive shows and films out
there. So, Dan Harmon calls this a story
embryo because it contains the essential
DNA of any good narrative. It's broken
down into eight simple logical steps.
Step number one is you, aka the comfort
zone. Firstly, you meet a character in
their zone of comfort. This is their
normal world, their before picture. It
doesn't have to be a happy place, per
se, because we have characters like
Harry Potter, who was miserable in a
cupboard under the stairs, but it is
familiar. We see who they are before the
story messes with them. In Toy Story, we
see Woody enjoying his status as Andy's
favorite toy. This step establishes a
baseline. Step number two is need, aka
the itch. Something happens. The
character develops a need or a want and
this is the inciting incident that kicks
off the story. Woody's world is upended
by the arrival of Buzz Lightyear and now
he needs to get back on top. This want
is the engine of the story. But often
what a character thinks they want isn't
what they truly need. And that gap is
where the best character development
happens. Step number three is go, aka
crossing the threshold. The character
acts on their need and enters an
unfamiliar situation. They cross the
threshold from their normal world into
the unknown. And this is the point of no
return. For instance, when Luke
Skywalker finds his aunt and uncle
murdered, his decision to leave with
Obi-Wan isn't just a choice. His old
life is gone. The protagonist has to be
an active participant here. They have to
choose to go. Now, step four is search,
aka the road of trials. So, now in an
unfamiliar world, the character has to
adapt. This is the search, a series of
escalating trials where they are tested
and forced to learn. Things get
complicated. They stumble. They fail.
They try new things. And this is where
the protagonist gradually starts to
change, even if they don't realize it.
For Woody and Buzz, this is getting
trapped at Sid's house and enduring his
destructive wrath. Now, step number five
is find, aka getting the thing. The
character finds what they were looking
for. They get the thing that they wanted
back in step two. And this is often a
false victory. Luke and Han rescue
Princess Leia, but now they're stuck on
the Death Star. Woody and Buzz finally
bond, but they're still lost. They got
what they wanted, but the story is far
from over. In fact, it just got more
complicated. Now, step number six is
take, aka paying the price. Getting what
they wanted comes at a heavy price. This
is a low point. The dark knight of the
soul. A mentor dies, a plan backfires,
and a terrible truth is revealed.
Obi-Wan sacrifices himself so that
others can't escape. This is where the
character is stripped of their illusions
and forced to confront the true cost of
their journey, and victory always has a
price tag. Now, step number seven is the
return, aka the journey home. The
character starts the journey back to
their familiar world, but they are the
same person who left. They bring what
they found, aka a person, an item, or a
lesson, back across the threshold. And
this return is often a final chase or
escape. In Toy Story, Woody and Buzz use
a rocket to fly back to the moving
truck, finally returning to Andy's room.
Step number eight is change, aka the new
normal. The character returns, but they
have changed. The world around them
might have changed, too. They now exist
in a new changed equilibrium. Woody
learns to share the spotlight and
coexist with Buzz, and Luke has evolved
from a whiny farm boy into a hero of the
rebellion. The character is back where
they started, but the journey has given
that starting point a new meaning. That
change is the entire point. So, where
